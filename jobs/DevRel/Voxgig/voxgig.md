## Company

Voxgig is a small, distributed team doing two things: building an open-source SDK generator, and running developer relations for API-first companies. The DevRel work is client-side, so this role sits at the intersection of both halves: hands-on with a real open-source product, and writing about APIs for developers.

Two roles were posted at the same time (11 and 15 September 2026): this Junior DevRel Engineer, and a Software Engineer, Compilers and Programming Languages in Waterford, Ireland.

## Role facts

- 100% remote, anywhere in the world
- Contract, part time or full time
- Posted 11 September 2026
- Salary: not published. "We are happy to work out a rate that works for both of us, depending on the particular circumstances and your level of experience. Our general contract rate policy is to match or exceed the market rate."

## What you'll do

Across code, content, community, and developer experience. "Depending on your strengths and interests":

- Build example applications, demos, integrations, and starter projects.
- Write tutorials, technical guides, documentation, and other developer content.
- Help developers using the product, and answer technical questions.
- Participate in relevant developer communities and conversations.
- Experiment with different ways of helping developers discover and understand the product.
- Turn new product features into examples and content developers can actually use.
- Identify confusing parts of the developer experience, and suggest ways to improve them.
- Gather feedback from developers, and bring useful insights back to product and engineering.
- Contribute to open-source projects and developer ecosystems relevant to the work.
- Over time, explore video, livestreams, workshops, events, community programs, and developer partnerships.

"We do not expect you to arrive knowing how to do all of this. Part of the role is learning what you are good at, and developing your skills as a DevRel practitioner."

## Who they're looking for

Technically capable enough to build software and understand how developers work. Not looking for years of experience, but comfortable writing code, using APIs, Git and GitHub, debugging, and learning unfamiliar technologies.

The listed backgrounds include: a junior engineer who enjoys writing and teaching, an open-source contributor, a technical writer who can code, a community member or moderator, a developer who creates tutorials/videos/demos, a bootcamp or CS graduate with a strong public body of work, or someone already doing DevRel informally who wants to make it their career.

"We care much more about what you have done than what your job title has been."

## The most important requirement

Evidence of DevRel-adjacent activity, listed as: open-source contributions, technical blog posts or tutorials, GitHub projects with thoughtful documentation, helping people in Discord/Slack/forums/Stack Overflow/Reddit, meetups, technical talks/workshops/livestreams/videos, developer documentation, sample applications or demos, hackathons, contributions to developer tooling, educational content.

"It does not have to be polished. You do not need a large audience. And none of it needs to have been part of a paid job."

"We would rather see a small GitHub project with an excellent README, or three useful technical articles, than a CV full of impressive-sounding titles."

## Do not need

Previous professional DevRel experience. A DevRel or Developer Advocate title. A CS degree. A large social following. Conference speaking. Thousands of GitHub followers. A personal brand.

## Good fit signals

Curious, learns new tech fast. Enjoys both coding and communicating. Explains technical ideas clearly without unnecessary complexity. Likes helping people solve problems. Comfortable working independently in a remote environment. Takes initiative rather than waiting for a detailed spec. Comfortable publishing publicly. Cares about the developer experience of software. Wants a career in DevRel.

## Location and working style

100% remote, open to candidates globally. Working asynchronously much of the time, so strong written communication and self-management are important.

## How to apply

Use the form on the jobs page. They ask for:

1. A short introduction, and why DevRel interests you
2. Something you have built (GitHub repo, open-source contribution, demo, application, other technical project)
3. Something showing how you help or explain things to developers (blog post, README, documentation, community contribution, video, talk, tutorial, forum answer)
4. Links to GitHub and anywhere else the work is visible
5. Optional CV ("welcome, but it will not be the most important part")

> "If you use AI for your application, and we could not tell, we will count that as a plus point."

One application per role. Or email info@voxgig.com with the same things.

## Notes

- The AI line is unusual and worth taking at face value. A small team that assumes applications are AI-drafted and prefers candidates who use it well. Lower bar than it looks; answer it directly rather than hiding it.
- No salary band. Negotiate from a number you set before replying, not after.
- Two roles open simultaneously at a small distributed team. Thin bench if it goes wrong.
- The SDK generator product means the DevRel work has a real subject to write about, not a vague "our platform" story.

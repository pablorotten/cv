When I read the line in your posting saying "I would love to get into Developer Relations, but every DevRel job asks for previous DevRel experience", I thought: this is exactly my situation.

I have been doing DevRel work inside a software job for the last five years without the title, and I want it to be the job.

At N-SIDE, my current company, I split my time between building an API product and helping customers use it. I designed and shipped around 20 API endpoints on top of a TYK gateway, wrote all the external documentation for them, and recorded screencasts to walk clients through integrations. When customers got stuck, I answered the technical emails myself, and I trained 50+ consultants internally. That work cut the number of support requests and led to the API becoming a product line of its own.

I also built and presented demos to potential clients, and supported the sales team during technical presales.

The part I enjoyed was always the explaining. Taking something I or my team had just built and working out how to say it so someone else could use it. So DevRel is not a change of direction for me, it is doing more of the thing I already end up doing anyway.

I know this is a junior role and I would be coming in with more years of experience than you are probably paying for. What I would bring is someone who can already write a tutorial, record a demo, and answer a developer question without needing a run-up.

On the AI question: yes, I used AI to draft parts of this. I do this every day to boost and polish my work, learn things, or challenge my ideas. But I never leave the creative part to it, and when I did, I regretted it a lot!
